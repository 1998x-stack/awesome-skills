# Example Scoping Questions

Examples of how the Think Phase generates scoping questions for different topic types. These demonstrate the question design principles — each question should resolve genuine ambiguity and change what you search for.

---

## Example 1: Technical Topic

**User prompt**: "Research retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)"

### Generated Questions

**Q1: Research Depth**
- Survey/overview — what RAG is and how it works at a high level
- Working knowledge — enough to design and implement a RAG system
- Expert-level — comprehensive understanding including edge cases and optimization (Recommended)
- State-of-the-art — cutting edge techniques, latest papers, bleeding edge

**Q2: Focus Area**
- Architecture patterns — how to design RAG pipelines (chunking, retrieval, reranking)
- Evaluation — how to measure RAG quality and debug failures
- Comparison with alternatives — RAG vs fine-tuning vs long context vs tool use
- Production deployment — scaling, latency, cost optimization

**Q3: Output Format**
- Technical deep-dive with implementation details and code examples (Recommended)
- Comparison matrix of RAG approaches and frameworks
- Executive briefing for technical leadership
- Research report with academic citations

**Q4: Technology Scope**
- Framework-agnostic (conceptual patterns)
- Focused on a specific framework (LangChain, LlamaIndex, etc.)
- Cloud-provider specific (AWS Bedrock, GCP Vertex, Azure)

## Example 2: Market Research Topic

**User prompt**: "Research the AI code assistant market"

### Generated Questions

**Q1: Focus**
- Competitive landscape — who are the players and how do they compare (Recommended)
- Market sizing — how big is the market and where is it heading
- Technology differentiation — what technical approaches distinguish products
- User adoption — how developers actually use these tools

**Q2: Scope**
- All AI coding tools (copilots, agents, chat, IDE integrations)
- Only AI coding agents (autonomous, multi-step tools)
- Only IDE-integrated copilots (inline completion, chat sidebar)

**Q3: Depth**
- Quick overview — key players and trends in 2-3 pages
- Working analysis — enough to inform a product strategy (Recommended)
- Deep competitive intelligence — detailed feature/pricing comparison

**Q4: Recency**
- Focus on current state (last 6 months) (Recommended)
- Include historical evolution (how we got here)
- Forward-looking (predictions, announced roadmaps)

## Example 3: Scientific Topic

**User prompt**: "Research the current understanding of protein folding"

### Generated Questions

**Q1: Angle**
- Computational methods — AlphaFold, ESMFold, and the AI revolution (Recommended)
- Biological fundamentals — the physics and chemistry of folding
- Drug discovery applications — how folding predictions are used in pharma
- Historical perspective — from Levinthal's paradox to today

**Q2: Depth**
- Accessible overview for a technically literate non-specialist
- Expert-level with references to key papers (Recommended)
- State-of-the-art including 2025-2026 developments

**Q3: Output**
- Research report with academic structure and citations (Recommended)
- Executive briefing for biotech leadership
- Comparison of computational approaches

## Example 4: Conceptual / Strategic Topic

**User prompt**: "Research how to build an effective engineering culture"

### Generated Questions

**Q1: Context**
- Early-stage startup (< 20 engineers)
- Growth-stage company (20-200 engineers) (Recommended)
- Large organization (200+ engineers)
- Remote/distributed team specifically

**Q2: Focus**
- Hiring and onboarding practices
- Technical practices (code review, testing, deployment)
- Team structure and communication patterns (Recommended)
- Performance management and career development

**Q3: Evidence Type**
- Case studies from successful companies
- Academic research on team effectiveness
- Practitioner advice from engineering leaders (Recommended)
- Data-driven analysis (DORA metrics, surveys)

## Anti-Patterns (Questions to Avoid)

**Too obvious**:
- "Should I include recent information?" (obviously yes)
- "Is accuracy important?" (always)

**Too narrow**:
- "Should I cover the Smith et al. 2024 paper?" (too specific for scoping)
- "Should I use arXiv or Google Scholar?" (implementation detail)

**Not actionable**:
- "Are you interested in this topic?" (they already asked)
- "How much time do you have?" (skill manages its own time)

The test: **would different answers to this question lead to meaningfully different research?** If all answers lead to the same search queries and structure, the question isn't worth asking.
