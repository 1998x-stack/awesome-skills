# Big History Lens

## Core proposition

The key question is not merely why a ruler succeeded or failed, but why a very large polity could maintain unity while struggling to develop durable forms of numerical management, legal-administrative clarity, and scalable institutional governance.

## Main analytical moves

### 1. Long duration
Read events through long-run accumulation, not only immediate episodes.

### 2. Structure over character
Shift from virtue and vice to institutions, incentives, and administrative design.

### 3. Numerical management
Ask whether the state could count, classify, compare, audit, and enforce at scale.

### 4. Center-local linkage
Ask how much the center actually knew, measured, and controlled, rather than how much it claimed.

### 5. System mismatch
Look for points where economic and social complexity outgrew inherited governing tools.

## Best-fit topics
- imperial governance
- chinese dynastic transitions
- late ming / qing stresses
- reform failure
- fiscal history
- comparative state capacity
- organizational diagnosis using historical analogy

## Common mistakes
- reducing everything to personalities
- mistaking moral language for causal explanation
- treating centralization as equivalent to high state capacity
- assuming modern bureaucracy is the only valid standard
