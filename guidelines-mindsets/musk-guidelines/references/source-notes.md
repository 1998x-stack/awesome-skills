# Source Notes

These notes summarize the public materials used to derive the skill.

## Primary themes extracted

### 1. First-principles reasoning over analogy
- In TED's 2013 summary of Musk's talk, he advises innovators to study physics and reason from first principles rather than by analogy.
- CNBC's summary of Musk's explanation uses the battery-cost example: instead of accepting historical battery pack prices, break the problem into material constituents and reason upward from fundamentals.

### 2. Five-step engineering order
- Everyday Astronaut's detailed write-up of the 2021 Starbase interview summarizes Musk's process in order:
  1. make the requirements less dumb
  2. delete the part or process
  3. simplify and optimize
  4. accelerate cycle time
  5. automate
- The article also captures two key nuances: requirements from smart people are especially dangerous because they go unquestioned, and a requirement should be traceable to a person rather than an abstract department.

### 3. Negative feedback and critique
- The TED 2013 summary says Musk's third tip to innovators is to pay attention to negative feedback, especially from friends.

### 4. Mission-driven commitment
- CBS News' republication of the 2012 60 Minutes interview and Snopes' fact-check both preserve Musk's line that when something is important enough, you do it even if the odds are not in your favor.
- This principle is adapted here as mission gravity: bold goals can justify discomfort and non-zero risk, but not abandonment of verification or safety.

## How the skill adapts the source material

The skill is not trying to imitate Musk's public persona. It extracts a reusable operating model:
- reduce the problem to fundamentals
- challenge inherited requirements
- delete before optimizing
- increase learning speed through short feedback loops
- automate only after the process is lean and stable
- keep a meaningful mission visible
